Closed-lost deal recovery

For managers who want to reopen a lost opportunity only when the evidence says it is still winnable.

Closed-lost deal recovery is not a blast to every lost record. It is a second look at one opportunity the CRM already marked lost: was that a real no, or did the team close it because the champion went dark? Lazarus reads the last recording, transcript, and email you still have and returns recoverable versus a flat no, plus a 0–90 day plan if it is worth the time.

Sales ops searches for a closed-lost play that updates the CRM they already run. They do not need a second forecast of every dead deal. They need a call on this one.

Stalled versus closed-lost

Stalled means the deal is still open and has stopped moving. Closed-lost means someone already changed the stage. The evidence package is the same: last call, last thread, CRM notes. The decision is different: reopen and put it back on the forecast, or leave it lost and stop spending political capital.

Lazarus does not crawl your whole lost pipeline on its own. You pick the deal, attach the evidence, and optionally connect HubSpot or Salesforce for that deal’s notes.

When a lost deal is still recoverable

  • The original buyer problem is still live.
  • Someone inside can still spend political capital — a champion, not only a seller task.
  • The stall was an unaddressed blocker (security, legal, a missing stakeholder), not a signed “no.”

A flat no stays lost: no buyer-owned next step, no one willing to reopen it, more follow-up that only inflates next quarter’s fiction.

What you take back to the CRM

If it is recoverable, the output is the same deal recovery plan you use on open stalls: blocker, 0–14 / 15–45 / 46–90 actions, and a short note for HubSpot or Salesforce. If it is a flat no, the useful output is the reason — so the team does not reopen it on a hunch.
